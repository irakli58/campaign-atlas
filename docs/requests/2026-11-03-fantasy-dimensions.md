ATLAS — Fantasy Dimensions, Planes of Existence & Lore-Accurate Planar World Generation
Claude, I want to expand Atlas's Fantasy/D&D campaign generation system to support entirely different dimensions and planes of existence, such as the Nine Hells of Baator, the Abyss, the Feywild, the Shadowfell, the Elemental Planes, and other fantasy realms.
I want this to be much more than changing the colors of an ordinary world map.
Each dimension should have its own geography, atmosphere, physical laws, environmental hazards, civilizations, creatures, lore, and visual identity.
The system should reuse Atlas's existing world generation technology, but adapt it to generate environments that don't necessarily follow the laws of the Material Plane.
For example, the Nine Hells should not simply be a normal world map with red terrain. Its geography, settlements, inhabitants, and structure should reflect its established D&D lore.
1. Add a Dimensions Tab
Create a dedicated Dimensions or Planes of Existence tab inside Fantasy campaigns.
The DM should be able to see all dimensions connected to their campaign.
There should be an Add Dimension button.
When clicked, the DM should have three options:

1. Generate from Existing Lore — Enter the name of an established fantasy dimension, such as the Nine Hells of Baator, and Atlas researches and generates it according to the selected setting's lore.
2. Create a Custom Dimension — The DM describes an original dimension and Atlas generates it.
3. Random Dimension — Atlas invents an original fantasy dimension, including its geography, inhabitants, physical laws, and history.

The DM should also be able to edit, rename, duplicate, or remove dimensions without affecting the Material Plane or other campaign maps.
2. AI-Guided Dimension Creation
When a DM selects Add Dimension, I want Atlas's AI to help them create it.
For example, the DM might type:
"Nine Hells of Baator."
Atlas should recognize that this is an established D&D plane and identify the relevant lore.
It should then ask any necessary questions, such as:

* Which D&D setting or edition should the dimension follow?
* Should Atlas follow established lore strictly or allow custom changes?
* Should all nine layers be generated or only a selected layer?
* What level of detail does the DM want?

Don't overwhelm the DM with unnecessary questions. If the name and campaign settings already provide enough information, use those details.
For an original dimension, Atlas could ask for a short description:
"A dimension of endless floating islands surrounded by magical storms, ruled by ancient dragons."
The AI should then expand that idea into a coherent, explorable realm.
Before generating an established dimension, research its actual lore rather than relying entirely on general AI knowledge.
3. Lore-Accurate Generation
This is extremely important.
For established D&D dimensions, Atlas should research their geography, cosmology, inhabitants, rulers, settlements, environmental conditions, and planar rules.
Use the campaign's selected D&D edition and setting as the primary reference.
Consult authoritative, legally accessible sources where available, and distinguish official lore from community interpretations, older editions, and original AI additions.
Do not assume that lore from different editions is always compatible.
If sources disagree, explain the differences and let the DM choose.
If a location or geographical detail is not established in the source material, Atlas may generate original content that fits the setting, but it should clearly distinguish generated additions from established canon.
For example, when generating the Nine Hells, Atlas should understand that Baator consists of nine distinct layers, not a single ordinary continent.
The generator should preserve established relationships between those layers, their rulers, important locations, and their defining characteristics.
It should not invent contradictory lore and present it as official.
Allow the DM to deliberately override canon if they want a homebrew interpretation.
4. Multi-Layered Dimensions
Some dimensions contain multiple distinct layers, regions, or interconnected realities.
Atlas should support this natively.
For example, the Nine Hells of Baator contain nine layers:

1. Avernus
2. Dis
3. Minauros
4. Phlegethos
5. Stygia
6. Malbolge
7. Maladomini
8. Cania
9. Nessus

These should not be generated as nine ordinary countries on the same map.
Each layer should be able to have its own map, geography, settlements, environment, and rules.
The Dimensions tab should show the overall structure of the plane and allow the DM to select individual layers.
Clicking a layer should open its corresponding generated map.
The system should also support dimensions with infinite expanses, floating islands, disconnected regions, nested realms, or unusual spatial structures.
Do not force every dimension into the same planet-shaped geography model.
5. Dimension-Specific World Generation
Take Atlas's existing world generation systems and make them adaptable to fantasy dimensions.
The generator should support environments such as:

* Endless volcanic wastelands.
* Rivers of fire.
* Frozen infernal landscapes.
* Floating islands.
* Infinite forests.
* Shadow-covered kingdoms.
* Magical oceans.
* Crystal deserts.
* Impossible mountain ranges.
* Underground worlds.
* Planes of perpetual storms.
* Elemental landscapes.
* Regions with altered gravity.
* Non-Euclidean or magically distorted spaces.

The generation system should choose appropriate geography based on the dimension's lore and physical rules.
For example, a plane of fire should not automatically generate Earth-like forests, rivers, and oceans.
A dimension made of floating islands should use a suitable spatial representation rather than forcing those islands onto an ordinary continental map.
Reuse existing terrain, city, cave, dungeon, and interior generators wherever they make sense.
Create specialized generation components only when the existing systems cannot represent the required environment.
6. Unique Color Palettes for Every Dimension
Every dimension should have its own distinct visual identity.
Add a dimension-specific color palette system.
For example:

* Nine Hells: Different palettes for different layers, including infernal reds, ash-gray wastelands, volcanic oranges, poisonous greens, or icy blues where appropriate.
* Feywild: Rich, unusual vegetation colors, magical lighting, and vibrant environments.
* Shadowfell: Desaturated landscapes, muted colors, dark forests, and oppressive atmospheric effects.
* Elemental Plane of Fire: Glowing lava, black volcanic rock, ash, and intense heat.
* Elemental Plane of Water: Deep oceanic colors, underwater lighting, and aquatic terrain.
* Elemental Plane of Air: Cloud formations, open skies, floating terrain, and atmospheric effects.

These are starting points, not universal rules.
Colors should reflect the specific dimension's lore and environment.
For example, not every layer of the Nine Hells should look red.
The DM should be able to choose from suggested palettes, customize individual terrain colors, or ask AI to generate a palette based on a description.
Changing a palette should not alter the underlying geography or destroy existing map details.
Also allow different regions or layers within the same dimension to use different palettes.
7. Different Physical and Magical Laws
Fantasy dimensions shouldn't necessarily obey normal physics.
Introduce a configurable system of planar rules.
Possible properties include:

* Gravity direction and strength.
* Whether the dimension has a sky, horizon, or conventional surface.
* Time flow relative to the Material Plane.
* Day and night cycles.
* Temperature and environmental conditions.
* Magical effects and restrictions.
* Planar alignment or supernatural influences where relevant.
* Travel and teleportation restrictions.
* Environmental damage and survival conditions.
* Special effects on creatures or spellcasting.

For established dimensions, these rules should come from the selected setting's lore and game mechanics.
For custom dimensions, the DM can define them.
Separate visual effects, narrative rules, and actual gameplay mechanics so they don't interfere with each other.
Do not invent official D&D rules when none exist.
8. Dimension-Specific Civilizations and Inhabitants
Each dimension should have appropriate creatures, factions, settlements, and civilizations.
For example, the Nine Hells might contain infernal fortresses, devil-controlled territories, military encampments, and other locations consistent with the chosen lore.
The Feywild might have powerful courts, magical settlements, and unusual inhabitants.
The Shadowfell might have its own settlements, ruins, and supernatural factions.
Use Atlas's existing NPC generation, faction simulation, city generation, and settlement systems.
However, adapt their behavior to the dimension.
A devil-controlled infernal city should not behave economically or politically like an ordinary medieval farming village.
Consider the dimension's social structure, resources, conflicts, hierarchy, and methods of transportation.
Allow DMs to add their own creatures, settlements, factions, and rulers.
9. Planar Travel and Connections
Dimensions should be connected through a dedicated planar travel system.
Possible connections include:

* Magical portals.
* Planar gates.
* Rituals.
* Spells.
* Hidden passages.
* Interdimensional crossroads.
* Special locations that connect multiple realms.

The DM should be able to create and edit these connections.
For example, a portal inside a city on the Material Plane might lead directly to a particular location in Avernus.
Players should be able to travel through the portal and arrive at the corresponding map location.
Support connections between layers of the same dimension as well.
Travel restrictions should follow the selected setting's rules or the DM's custom rules.
Do not automatically make every dimension accessible from every other dimension.
10. Integrate With Existing Atlas Maps
Atlas already supports world maps, clickable cities, 3D city views, building interiors, caves, and other location maps.
Preserve that functionality.
When the DM opens a dimension, they should see its world or regional map.
Clicking a settlement should open its existing city generation system, adapted to the dimension's architecture and environment.
Clicking buildings should continue to open their interiors.
For example, an infernal fortress might use different architecture and assets from a normal medieval castle.
Use the existing 2D and 3D asset libraries, expanding them only where necessary.
Do not duplicate systems that already work.
11. AI Writer Councils and Dimension Lore
Connect dimension generation to Atlas's existing AI Writer Councils.
When generating an established dimension, use a lore-focused research and consistency process.
The AI should understand the dimension's history, important figures, geography, factions, and narrative themes.
For original dimensions, the Fantasy Writer Council can help develop their mythology, civilizations, conflicts, and unique characteristics.
The AI DM Copilot should also understand the active dimension.
For example, when players enter the Nine Hells, the Copilot should use the relevant planar lore, environmental conditions, inhabitants, and campaign history.
It should not accidentally describe the environment as an ordinary fantasy kingdom.
Maintain consistent knowledge of where the players are and which planar rules apply.
12. Random Dimension Generation
I also want Atlas to invent completely original dimensions.
When generating a random dimension, it should create:

* A name.
* A central concept or theme.
* A cosmological structure.
* Distinct geography.
* A unique color palette.
* Environmental and magical rules.
* Native creatures.
* Civilizations and factions, if appropriate.
* Important locations.
* History and mythology.
* Major conflicts.
* Methods of entering and leaving.
* Interesting adventure opportunities.

These characteristics should be connected logically.
For example, if the dimension consists of floating islands and perpetual storms, its settlements, transportation, architecture, and cultures should reflect those conditions.
Don't simply randomize unrelated characteristics.
I want each generated dimension to feel like a coherent fantasy setting.
13. Editing and Customization
The DM should have complete control over generated dimensions.
Allow them to:

* Rename dimensions.
* Edit their descriptions and lore.
* Change their color palettes.
* Add or remove regions and layers.
* Modify geography.
* Add custom locations.
* Create cities, dungeons, and other maps.
* Add NPCs, creatures, and factions.
* Change planar rules.
* Create portals between dimensions.
* Regenerate individual regions without replacing the entire dimension.
* Lock manually edited content against regeneration.

Any AI-generated changes affecting established campaign canon should require DM approval.
14. Modular Integration
This system should be optional.
A DM running a simple fantasy campaign shouldn't have to interact with planar generation.
The Dimensions tab can remain hidden or inactive when the feature is disabled.
Use Atlas's existing modular feature settings.
Enabling or disabling dimensions should not delete generated maps or campaign data.
15. Research and Validation
Before implementation, research established D&D cosmology and planar geography.
Study the differences between the Great Wheel cosmology, other published cosmological models, and setting-specific interpretations.
Research the Nine Hells, Abyss, Feywild, Shadowfell, Elemental Planes, Astral Plane, and other relevant locations.
Understand which planes have conventional geography and which require fundamentally different map representations.
Also examine how other fantasy settings handle multiple dimensions.
For established lore, provide references and clearly identify any original generated additions.
For original dimensions, evaluate whether the generated geography, inhabitants, and physical rules are internally consistent.
Test several dimensions, including:

* The Nine Hells with all nine layers.
* A Feywild-style realm.
* A Shadowfell-style realm.
* An elemental dimension.
* A completely original random dimension.

Compare their maps and make sure they don't simply look like recolored versions of the same world.
16. Implementation Plan and Approval
Before implementing this system, inspect Atlas's existing Fantasy world generator, map navigation, AI Writer Councils, campaign data structures, and modular feature settings.
Then present a proposed implementation plan explaining:

1. How the Dimensions tab will work.
2. How established dimensions will be researched and generated accurately.
3. How multiple layers and unconventional geography will be represented.
4. How existing world generation will be reused.
5. How dimension-specific palettes and environmental rules will work.
6. How planar travel connects different maps.
7. How lore consistency will be maintained.
8. What additional features you recommend.

Do not implement major changes until I review and approve the plan.
The goal is to make Atlas capable of generating entire fantasy cosmologies, not just individual worlds.
I want a DM to be able to type "Generate the Nine Hells of Baator" and have Atlas understand what that means, research the appropriate lore, create the nine distinct layers, generate appropriate maps and locations, and connect everything into an explorable campaign setting.
And if the DM types "Generate a dimension where time flows backward and cities float above an endless ocean," Atlas should be able to create that too.
Every dimension should feel like a genuinely different reality, not just an ordinary map with a different color filter.
