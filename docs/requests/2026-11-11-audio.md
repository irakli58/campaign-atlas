ATLAS — Realistic Soundboard, Free Sound Libraries & Local AI Narrator
Claude, I want you to significantly improve Atlas's existing music player, soundboard, and narration systems.
Right now, the soundboard doesn't sound realistic enough because you've attempted to recreate many of the sounds programmatically.
I want you to investigate whether we can use actual recorded sound effects, environmental ambience, and professionally produced audio instead of trying to synthesize everything ourselves.
I also want to investigate whether Atlas can have a high-quality, natural-sounding AI narrator that runs locally on the user's computer, preferably for free and without requiring external AI APIs.
Please inspect the existing implementation before making changes.
1. Replace Unrealistic Sounds With Real Recordings
First, audit the current soundboard.
Identify which sounds are synthesized programmatically and which are actual recordings.
I want realistic sound effects for tabletop RPG campaigns, including:
Environmental ambience

* Rain, thunderstorms, and wind.
* Forests, birds, insects, and wildlife.
* Rivers, waterfalls, and oceans.
* Caves, underground ambience, and dripping water.
* Fireplaces, campfires, and crackling flames.
* Snowstorms and blizzards.
* Deserts and sandstorms.

Fantasy environments

* Medieval taverns.
* Crowded marketplaces.
* Blacksmith workshops.
* Castle interiors.
* Dungeon ambience.
* Magical portals.
* Spell effects.
* Dragon roars and monster sounds.
* Sword fighting and armor movement.

Sci-Fi environments

* Spaceship engines.
* Reactor hums.
* Space station ambience.
* Computer terminals.
* Electronic beeps.
* Airlocks.
* Mechanical doors.
* Spaceship alarms.
* Distress signals.
* Radio static and communications.
* Machinery and industrial environments.

Horror and investigation

* Footsteps.
* Doors opening and closing.
* Creaking floors.
* Distant screams.
* Strange noises.
* Unsettling drones.
* Heartbeats.
* Abandoned building ambience.
* Radio interference.
* Creature sounds.

These are examples, not an exhaustive list.
I want a large, varied collection of convincing sounds rather than a handful of obviously synthetic effects.
2. Investigate Free Online Sound Libraries
Research whether you can legally download and integrate high-quality sounds from existing online libraries.
Investigate sources such as:

* Freesound — https://freesound.org
* Pixabay Sound Effects — https://pixabay.com/sound-effects/
* Sonniss GDC audio bundles — https://sonniss.com
* OpenGameArt — https://opengameart.org
* Internet Archive — https://archive.org
* Other reputable free or open-license sound libraries.

Determine which sources allow sounds to be downloaded and redistributed as part of an application.
Do not assume that free to download means free to redistribute commercially.
Check licensing, attribution requirements, redistribution restrictions, and whether automated downloading is permitted.
Prefer CC0, public-domain, or otherwise appropriately licensed audio.
If a source requires attribution, design a way for Atlas to retain and display that information.
Do not scrape websites or bypass access restrictions.
If automated downloading is permitted, investigate whether you can download and organize suitable audio assets directly.
If not, propose a legitimate alternative.
3. Build a Proper Audio Asset Library
I want Atlas to have a well-organized sound library.
Each sound should have metadata such as:

* Name.
* Category.
* Description.
* Duration.
* Loop compatibility.
* Volume recommendations.
* Source.
* License.
* Attribution requirements.
* Tags.

Allow DMs to search and filter sounds.
For example, searching for "abandoned spaceship" should help find relevant ambience, machinery, alarms, and environmental sounds.
Reuse the existing soundboard UI wherever possible.
Avoid downloading duplicate audio files.
4. Improve Sound Quality and Playback
The soundboard should sound natural and professional.
Investigate improvements such as:

* Seamless audio looping.
* Smooth fade-in and fade-out.
* Crossfading between ambience tracks.
* Multiple simultaneous audio layers.
* Independent volume controls.
* Background ambience that continues during navigation.
* Spatial audio or stereo positioning where useful.
* Randomized variations of repeated effects.
* Avoiding clipping and excessive loudness.
* Audio normalization.
* Smooth transitions between environments.

For example, a thunderstorm could combine a continuous rain recording with occasional thunder effects.
A spaceship could combine engine ambience, ventilation, computer sounds, and occasional alerts.
However, don't overcomplicate the interface.
I want realistic audio with simple controls.
5. Investigate Free Local AI Narration
I also want Atlas to have a genuinely good narrator voice.
Right now, I'm not sure whether we can download a free AI voice model and run it locally.
Please investigate this thoroughly.
Research local text-to-speech systems such as:

* Piper TTS.
* Kokoro TTS.
* Coqui XTTS / XTTS-v2.
* StyleTTS2.
* Other modern open-source or freely available local TTS models.

Check whether these projects are actively maintained and whether their models are legally usable and redistributable.
Compare their voice quality, naturalness, emotional expressiveness, hardware requirements, inference speed, and installation complexity.
I want a narrator that sounds like an actual person telling a story, not an old-fashioned robotic text-to-speech system.
Ideally, it should support:

* Natural pacing.
* Appropriate pauses.
* Expressive intonation.
* Different speaking styles.
* Fantasy storytelling.
* Horror narration.
* Sci-Fi narration.
* Dialogue.
* Multiple character voices.
* Adjustable speaking speed.
* Consistent pronunciation of names.

Investigate whether emotional delivery can be controlled reliably rather than assuming every model supports it.
6. Local Narration Without API Costs
My preference is for narration to work entirely on the user's computer.
Ideally:

1. The user installs or downloads the voice model once.
2. Atlas loads the model locally.
3. The DM enters text or generates a story.
4. The narrator reads the text aloud.
5. No external API calls are required.

Determine whether this is practical for the current Atlas architecture.
If Atlas runs in a browser, investigate whether local inference through WebGPU, WebAssembly, or a local companion service would be appropriate.
If a desktop application is more practical, explain why.
Don't assume that downloading a voice model automatically makes it usable in a browser.
7. Narrator Voice Library
If technically feasible, I want a selection of narrator voices.
For example:

* Deep fantasy storyteller.
* Calm documentary narrator.
* Dark horror narrator.
* Mysterious investigator.
* Sci-Fi computer voice.
* Neutral narrator.
* Different character voices.

These should be genuinely different voice models or supported voice configurations, not merely pitch-shifted versions of the same recording.
Allow users to preview voices before selecting them.
If the models support it, let DMs adjust narration style, speed, and expressiveness.
Investigate whether we can download a small selection of high-quality voices and keep them available offline.
Only include models and voices with appropriate usage rights.
Do not clone real people's voices without permission.
8. Narration Integrated With Soundboard
I want the narrator and soundboard to work together.
For example, when narrating a scene inside a derelict spaceship:

* The narrator reads the scene description.
* The spaceship ambience continues in the background.
* A distant mechanical noise might play at a designated moment.
* The background audio automatically lowers while the narrator speaks.
* The ambience returns to its previous volume afterward.

This is called audio ducking.
I want you to investigate how to implement it smoothly.
Ideally, the DM should also be able to manually trigger narration and sound effects during a session.
Don't make every sound automatic. The DM should remain in control.

Investigate whether Atlas can support reusable soundscape presets.

Abandoned Space Station

Medieval Tavern

* Fireplace.

Haunted Mansion

* Wind.

* Distant footsteps.
* Subtle unsettling ambience.

Allow DMs to save custom combinations of sounds.
These presets should be reusable across campaigns through Atlas's existing library system.
10. Music Licensing
I also want you to investigate free, legally usable background music.
Look for ambient fantasy, horror, and Sci-Fi music that can be included in Atlas.
Pay particular attention to:

* Commercial redistribution rights.
* Attribution requirements.
* Looping quality.
* Audio file sizes.
* Offline playback.

Do not download copyrighted commercial soundtracks without permission.
Keep music and sound effects organized separately.
11. Performance and Storage
Evaluate how much storage the audio library and narrator models would require.
I don't want Atlas to become unnecessarily large.
Consider:

* Optional audio packs.
* Downloadable genre-specific sound libraries.
* Optional narrator model downloads.
* Audio compression.
* Caching.
* Lazy loading.
* Offline availability.

For example, a Fantasy DM shouldn't have to download several gigabytes of Sci-Fi sound effects.
Allow users to choose which sound and voice packs they want installed.

Before implementing anything, inspect the current:

* Music player.
* Soundboard.
* Sound generation.
* Narrator.
* Audio playback.
* Campaign integration.
* Settings.
* Asset storage.

Identify what already works and what needs improvement.
Don't rebuild the entire audio system unnecessarily.
Reuse existing functionality wherever possible.

My priority is to make Atlas sound like a professionally produced tabletop RPG experience without requiring expensive AI subscriptions or constant internet access.
I want convincing environmental audio, realistic sound effects, atmospheric music, and a narrator that can deliver immersive storytelling naturally.
Prefer real recordings for sound effects and high-quality local TTS for narration. Don't recreate sounds artificially when better, legally usable recordings already exist.
